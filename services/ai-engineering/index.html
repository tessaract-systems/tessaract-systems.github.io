Capabilities

What we build

Six things we ship most often. Each one reaches real users or it isn't finished.

Agents & multi-agent systems

Agents that carry out real work across your systems — triage, research, reconciliation, drafting — with explicit tool access, defined escalation paths and a human in the loop wherever the cost of being wrong is high.

RAG & enterprise search

Retrieval over your own documents, tickets and wikis, built so answers cite their source. Chunking, embedding and re-ranking tuned against a real question set, not a generic benchmark.

Voice & conversational AI

Assistants that answer the phone, hold a conversation at human latency, handle interruption, and pass the caller to a person with the context already gathered.

Document intelligence

Extraction from invoices, contracts, forms and scans into structured data your systems can act on, with confidence thresholds that route the uncertain cases to a person.

Process automation

The repetitive judgement work that sits between your systems: classification, routing, data entry, follow-up. Automated where the rules are clear, assisted where they aren't.

Copilots for your teams

Internal assistants grounded in your own definitions, so finance, support and operations get answers that match the numbers in your reports.

The hard part

What makes it production-grade

The gap between a working demo and a system you'd let near a customer is mostly this list.

An evaluation set, before launch
We build a graded question set from your real cases and score every change against it. Without this, "it seems better" is the only measure anyone has.
Guardrails with teeth
Input and output filtering, tool permissions scoped to the task, and hard limits on what an agent can do unattended. An agent that can act needs boundaries that are enforced, not prompted.
Observability from day one
Every call traced, every tool invocation logged, every cost attributed. When something goes wrong in month three you need the transcript, not a theory.
Model portability
We keep the prompt, retrieval and orchestration layers independent of the provider, so a price change or a better model is a config change rather than a rebuild.
